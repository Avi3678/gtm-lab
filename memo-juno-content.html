Juno / Social & content

Let the comments write the content calendar.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: for a chronic-illness assistant, the most trustworthy content is built from what the community already says in the comments, not from a brand voice deck.

What I noticed

Juno is an AI personal health assistant for people living with chronic conditions, and its Social Media & Content Intern owns TikTok and Instagram end to end: ideas, filming, editing, publishing, and community engagement. The posting is unusually honest about the audience: a health condition shapes energy, relationships, work, and confidence, not just symptoms. Anthropic features Juno as a Claude customer, so the product is real and in production.

I'd test a comment-to-content loop: every week, mine the questions and phrasings people actually use under Juno's posts and in chronic-illness communities, then turn the three most repeated ones into short videos that answer them plainly. Measure saves and shares, not views, because for this audience a save means 'this is me.' That is a format hypothesis, not a claim that Juno's current content is off.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Read existing posts, comments, and community spaces. Learn the language members use for flare days, fatigue, and appointments, and agree on boundaries for what Juno should never joke about.

Days 8-20. Run the first comment-to-content cycle: source three questions, script and film three videos, publish, and log performance by saves and replies.

Days 21-30. Double down on the best-performing question type, build a repeatable sourcing doc, and hand the team a two-week calendar grounded in real community questions.

Building Prism AI taught me that users tell you what to make if you read what they actually write. I would bring that listening habit to an audience that deserves to be spoken with, not marketed at.
