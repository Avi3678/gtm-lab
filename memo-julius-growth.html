Julius AI / Growth

Start with the question behind the board slide.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Julius's example decks make recurring business decisions a useful starting point for growth content.

What I noticed

The homepage shows examples including marketing-channel performance, a quarterly business review, a survey readout and an investor update. These are recognizable work products, not only a list of AI features. They suggest a content angle anchored in the meeting a user needs to prepare for and the question that meeting must answer.

I'd test one reproducible story from a public dataset to a decision-ready marketing update. The content would show the original question, the analysis checks and what the final slide does not establish. My hypothesis is that a familiar job plus visible verification could attract better-fit users than a polished output alone. The homepage does not tell me which use cases actually convert.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Ask the team which user group and activation event matter. Review approved examples and learn where users need help turning an answer into a useful deliverable.

Days 8-20. Produce one source-linked walkthrough for that group. Show assumptions, a chart check and the final communication. Create a short clip and a written version from the same work.

Days 21-30. Distribute through an approved channel. Compare qualified signups and completion of the agreed useful action, then collect feedback before extending the series.

I've worked on content engines at Flywheelr and presented portfolio research in the Capitol Hill Challenge. I'd connect analysis with clear communication, then follow the team's actual priorities.
