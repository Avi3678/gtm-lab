Homebase / CEO office, strategy

Hourly teams run on texts, not dashboards.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Homebase's strategic advantage is how deeply it sits in the daily routine of hourly work. Corp dev and strategy projects should start from that workflow intimacy.

What I noticed

Homebase is the operating system for small businesses with hourly teams: scheduling, time clocks, payroll, hiring, and team messaging in one app used by hundreds of thousands of local businesses. Its Office of the CEO Intern role spans strategic operations and corporate development, remote in the US, reporting toward the top of a company that still behaves like a startup.

I'd test a segment-deep-dive project: pick one vertical, coffee shops, salons, gyms, and map how their owners actually run the week inside Homebase versus outside it. The gaps between the two are the corp dev surface: integrations, partnerships, or features worth buying rather than building. That is a research hypothesis, not a claim that Homebase's roadmap misses these gaps.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product surface, the customer segments, and how the CEO office prioritizes projects. Read existing market and segment analyses.

Days 8-20. Run the first vertical deep-dive: usage patterns, adjacent tools, and the top three buy-or-partner opportunities, reviewed with the team.

Days 21-30. Turn the deep-dive into a decision memo with a clear recommendation, and outline the second vertical to study.

Strategy work I respect ends in a decision. In competition and in my own business, I learned to go from analysis to a call someone can act on, and I would bring that discipline to the CEO office.
