Campfire / Marketing

Show the close week before and after.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Campfire's proof is already numerical. The opportunity is to make those numbers legible as a week in a controller's life.

What I noticed

Campfire is the AI-native ERP for modern finance teams, and its customer evidence is unusually concrete: PostHog shaving five to six days off its close, Klarity saving 80+ hours monthly, Replit scaling revenue 20x without added finance headcount, Flex cutting close times 70%. The marketing generalist internship spans content, campaigns, events, research, sales enablement and SEO, reporting into the marketing team rather than the founders.

I'd test a recurring close-diary format: one approved finance team's close week before and after Campfire, told in hours, handoffs and reconciliations rather than features. A controller should be able to check the arithmetic, and every number stays tied to approved customer evidence. It doubles as sales enablement, which the role explicitly owns. That is a format hypothesis, not a claim that Campfire's current case studies undersell the product.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product surface, the approved customer evidence and the voice of the existing content. Pick one customer story and agree on the diary format with the marketing team.

Days 8-20. Draft the first close diary, adapt it into a newsletter section and a sales one-pager, and ship through the normal review path.

Days 21-30. Support one webinar or customer dinner end to end. Report engagement and pipeline influence on the diary, and propose the next two stories.

I have presented research to financial executives (SIFMA, second nationally) and run a business on weekly numbers. Finance readers notice when numbers are decoration; I write so they are not.
