Founding go-to-market

Hardware teams buy back weeks, not software.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Ohm helps engineering teams accelerate hardware development and testing, and the founding GTM intern's real job is translating acceleration into the unit hardware leads actually budget around: weeks of schedule. Every asset should start and end in that unit.

What I noticed

Ohm (YC W23) helps engineering teams accelerate hardware development and testing, and it is hiring a Founding GTM Intern in San Francisco on contract, at $1K to $2.5K a month plus equity. Hardware buyers are conservative: they have been burned by tools that promised speed and delivered integration debt.

I'd test a schedule-first account motion: for each target hardware team, estimate the calendar cost of their current test loop from public signals, shipping cadence, job posts for test engineers, certification timelines, and open with the weeks at stake before mentioning the product. Measure meetings per account against a capability-first pitch. That is a messaging hypothesis, not a claim that Ohm's current outreach leads with features.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Shadow the founders' current sales conversations and log every way prospects describe delay in their own words.

Days 8-20. Build the first ten schedule-first account briefs and run them as the opening move; track reply and meeting rates against the existing pitch.

Days 21-30. Turn the two best conversations into written proof points the founders can reuse, and document the motion so the next intern inherits a system, not a hunch.

I placed 2nd nationally in the SIFMA competition, where the winning habit is converting vague advantages into numbers a skeptic can check. A summer 2027 internship as the founding GTM hire at a hardware acceleration company is exactly the translation work I want to learn.
