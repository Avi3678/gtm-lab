Content

Agent teams earn trust one deployment story at a time.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Surface Labs deploys agent teams for critical business operations, and its content should read like field reports: what the agents took over, what broke, what humans still handle. Operations leaders trust scars, not promises.

What I noticed

Surface Labs is an agentic data warehouse company that builds, stress tests, and deploys agent teams for critical business operations, and it is hiring an AI Content Intern, remote or San Francisco. In operations, every buyer has a story about automation that failed loudly, so credibility is the whole game.

I'd test a deployment-diary content series: one real workflow per post, the manual version's cost, what the agents do now, and the honest failure modes, written with the customer as the hero. Measure qualified inbound per story, not impressions. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim that Surface's current publishing lacks specifics.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Interview the team and one deployment's operators to collect the concrete before-and-after numbers.

Days 8-20. Publish the first two deployment diaries and test which operations audiences engage.

Days 21-30. Systematize the format into a repeatable series with a distribution checklist per post.

I grew Flywheelr to 100K+ LinkedIn impressions by writing specific, verifiable stories instead of brand adjectives. A summer 2027 internship writing deployment diaries for agent teams is exactly the content work I want to do.
