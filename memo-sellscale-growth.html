SellScale / Growth

The best demo of AI outbound is the outbound itself.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: SellScale sells agentic outbound, so its own outbound is the product demo; every campaign it runs on itself is proof or disproof, and growth work should treat it that way.

What I noticed

SellScale, a San Francisco startup backed by Pear.VC, Elad Gil, and operators from Ramp and Clay, describes itself as building the first agentic revenue org. Its live jobs board lists founding growth, product marketing, and GTM operations roles, and it posted a Growth Intern listing for San Francisco in recent weeks, a pattern of investing in its own go-to-market engine as the showcase.

I'd test an open-metrics motion: pick one narrow vertical, run SellScale's own agents on it end-to-end, and publish the build, targeting logic, message drafts, replies, meetings, honestly annotated. Buyers of outbound software are the hardest audience to impress and the easiest to win with receipts. Measure qualified meetings per published breakdown. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim that SellScale currently hides its numbers.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the agent system by running it on a test vertical, and study which of the company's existing campaigns and posts have produced real meetings.

Days 8-20. Run one full self-outbound campaign on a new vertical, from list to meetings, documenting every decision so it can be published.

Days 21-30. Publish the annotated campaign breakdown, compare engagement and inbound against prior content, and spec the next vertical run.

Selling to sellers means nothing generic survives, which is the most useful possible environment for learning growth. I would rather have my campaigns judged by operators than applauded by classmates.
