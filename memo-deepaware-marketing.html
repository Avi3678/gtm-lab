DeepAware AI / Marketing

Treat the monthly meetup as the top of funnel.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: DeepAware's community events are already its best demo channel. The marketing engine should be built around them, not beside them.

What I noticed

DeepAware runs the Robotics Center of Silicon Valley: 60+ robot platforms, hardware delivered across the US in 48 to 72 hours or picked up in SF, plus teleoperation data collection and reinforcement-learning environments. Customers include AI labs, research teams and event producers, and the team already hosts monthly community meetups. The marketing intern owns content across LinkedIn, X, YouTube and TikTok, community, events and launch campaigns.

I'd test turning each meetup into a full content cycle: one clip before (what we will show), one recap after (what actually happened, including what broke), and one attendee question answered in public. Physical AI has a credibility problem with polished renders; slightly imperfect real footage is the differentiator, because researchers can tell the difference. Measure meetup signups and inbound hardware inquiries, not views. That is a channel hypothesis, not a claim about what the team does today.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Attend the lab, meet the community regulars, and learn which robots and demos draw a crowd. Agree on the pre/post format and one metric per event.

Days 8-20. Run the cycle for one meetup: teaser, live coverage, recap, follow-up answer. Document the process so it repeats without me.

Days 21-30. Compare inquiries and signups against the prior month. Turn the best moment into a launch-style post and plan the next event's angle.

I build technical products myself (Prism AI, a security tool with a working demo loop), so I am comfortable filming real systems and honest about what it takes to make them behave.
