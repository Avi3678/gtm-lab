Amplify / Marketing

Give the educator one useful next step.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Amplify's broad product portfolio makes a specific educator question a better content starting point than a general product tour.

What I noticed

The company page describes core and supplemental curriculum alongside assessment and intervention across ELA, math and science. It connects formative assessment with targeted instruction. That offers a natural teaching sequence: what does an educator learn from a result, and what instructional question should they ask next? It does not mean a public marketing asset can diagnose an individual student's need.

I'd test a short, reviewed educator-facing guide around one team-selected question, with a clear link to an existing approved resource. It would explain the next step without inventing learning gains or treating a sample as a research finding. The hypothesis is that practical usefulness could encourage a more relevant follow-up; I do not know Amplify's content performance or current campaign priorities.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the priority educator audience, subject and campaign goal. Audit existing resources and evidence standards. Ask which questions teachers repeat and what language needs curriculum-team review.

Days 8-20. Draft one resource-led story and a few channel-specific versions. Keep product claims tied to approved evidence and avoid student-identifying information. Get instructional and marketing review before publication.

Days 21-30. Support an approved distribution test. Compare relevant resource use and the agreed next action, not reach alone. Collect questions from educators and use them to improve the next piece.

At Flywheelr I built content workflows behind 100K+ LinkedIn impressions, and I co-founded a financial-literacy initiative. I'd bring that production experience while learning from Amplify's educators rather than assuming what they need.
