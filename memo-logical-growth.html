Logical / Growth

Make the interruption you prevented the story.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: a deliberately quiet product needs content that shows the moment it saved, not another feature montage.

What I noticed

Logical is a two-person team building a proactive desktop AI that understands your context (meetings, inbox, what is on screen) and helps without being prompted. The founders describe their bar as craft: native apps that are fast, quiet and useful. The growth role owns video creative, the content engine across X, LinkedIn and YouTube, creator campaigns and small measured experiments.

I'd test a recurring format built around one interrupted task. Show the forty minutes a knowledge worker loses to context-switching on an ordinary morning, then the same morning with Logical surfacing the one thing that mattered. The contrast is the pitch, and the product stays quiet inside it, which matches how the product is meant to feel. Measure saves-to-signup rather than views. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim about what Logical's current growth work gets wrong.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Use the product daily. Learn what has already been tried, which channels carry signups today, and what the founders consider on-brand. Agree on one format and one activation metric.

Days 8-20. Ship two reviewed pieces in the interruption format: shoot, edit, publish, read the numbers. Start a shortlist of creators whose audiences match the prosumer user.

Days 21-30. Run one small experiment, such as a landing-page variant or a referral prompt tied to the content. Write a short review of what worked and what to double down on.

I am building Prism AI, a security tool that turns plain-English rules into tests, so I work close to a technical product every day. Running an e-commerce business taught me that traffic only matters if someone buys.
