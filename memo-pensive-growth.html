Pensive / Growth

Professors adopt tools that respect their judgment.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Pensive's growth motion should center the instructor's authority, because the fastest way to lose a professor is to make the AI look like it is grading for them instead of with them.

What I noticed

Pensive is an AI grader and tutor for higher education, built around instructor-led AI grading with personalized feedback, a small San Francisco team of about thirteen. It hired a Growth Intern for fall 2026, which suggests the team invests in growth interns and may again. Higher ed is a trust-driven sale: instructors fear losing control of standards more than they fear workload.

I'd test an instructor-first growth loop: short demos that start from a real rubric and show the AI applying the instructor's own criteria, with the instructor editing every suggestion. The asset is a two-minute 'your rubric, your standards, less time' video per discipline. Measure demo-to-pilot conversion by department. That is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim that Pensive's current pitch centers the AI.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the grading workflow, and current acquisition channels. Sit in on a demo or watch recordings to hear instructor objections firsthand.

Days 8-20. Produce two discipline-specific demo assets built on real rubrics. Test them in outbound to instructors and track replies.

Days 21-30. Report which framing earned pilots, refine the assets, and document the outreach sequence for the team to rerun.

As a Berkeley student I am the end user of tools like this, and I hear how both students and instructors talk about AI grading. That dual perspective is what I would bring to Pensive's growth work.
