Admisist AI / Content marketing

Admissions content works when it admits what students actually fear.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Admisist's content should start from the specific anxieties of application season - the blank essay page, the activity list that feels thin - because that is where students actually are when they search for help.

What I noticed

Admisist AI is an all-in-one AI toolkit for college admissions, built to help students through every part of the application. It posted a Marketing and Creative Content Intern role, remote within the United States, about a week ago - an early team investing in content where its users already are.

I'd test a fear-first content engine: short pieces that each take one real admissions anxiety (the essay with no topic, the interview, the safety-school list) and show it getting worked through honestly, with the product appearing as the method rather than the hero. Students share content that understands them, and application season has a hard calendar, so timing the content to deadlines is half the strategy. Measure signups per piece as deadlines approach. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim that Admisist's current content is generic.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Use the product through a mock application myself, map the admissions calendar for the next two quarters, and study which content channels students actually trust.

Days 8-20. Produce the first batch of fear-first pieces timed to the nearest deadlines, distribute in student communities, and track signup response.

Days 21-30. Compare formats and topics, align the calendar to the next deadline cluster, and document the repeatable content process.

I finished this process recently enough to remember exactly what the blank essay page feels like, and most of my friends are in it right now. Writing content for an audience I genuinely understand, on a deadline calendar I have personally lived, is a rare advantage for an intern to bring.
