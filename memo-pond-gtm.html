Social media & go-to-market

A marketplace of AI builders should market the builders.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Pond is an AI workforce marketplace with thousands of AI builders, and its social content should come from the supply side: real builders shipping real agents. Buyers trust a marketplace when they can see the people inside it.

What I noticed

Pond is an AI workforce marketplace covering lead generation and custom AI agents, with a community of about 7,000 AI builders, and its CEO posted in July 2026 that the team is hiring a social media and GTM intern on-site in San Francisco's FiDi. Marketplaces live or die on whether buyers believe the supply is real.

I'd test a builder-spotlight content engine: short profiles of builders on the platform and the agents they have shipped, what the agent does, what it replaced, what it earns, told concretely. Each post doubles as recruiting for supply and proof for demand. Measure inbound project posts per spotlight. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim that Pond's current channels lack proof.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Interview five active builders and draft the first spotlights in the voice buyers actually use when describing their problems.

Days 8-20. Publish the first series across the channels where ops and growth leads spend time, and track which stories drive project posts.

Days 21-30. Turn the best format into a weekly ritual with a repeatable interview kit any team member can run.

I grew Flywheelr to 100K+ LinkedIn impressions by making specific people and projects the story instead of generic brand content. A summer 2027 internship building a marketplace's voice from its builders is exactly the content work I want to do.
