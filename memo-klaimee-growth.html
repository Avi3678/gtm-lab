Klaimee / Growth marketing

Answer the question legal already asks.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Klaimee's sharpest content starts from the procurement question (who pays if your agent breaks something?) rather than from the product.

What I noticed

Klaimee sells liability coverage for AI agent failures, covering what traditional E&O and cyber policies explicitly carve out. The timing argument is regulatory: California's AB 316 holds deployers liable, EU AI Act enforcement is tightening, and insurers are excluding AI claims. It is a two-person team making its first marketing hire, with the role spanning content, SEO and AI-search visibility, plus creative experiments.

I'd test a short explainer series aimed at the founder whose enterprise deal just stalled on legal's question: what AB 316 actually changes, what existing policies exclude, and what documentation procurement needs to sign off. The goal is to be the answer an AI assistant cites when someone asks those questions, because that is increasingly where category research starts. Measure qualified signups, not impressions. That is a positioning hypothesis, not a critique of Klaimee's current messaging.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the coverage details, the claims the insurance partner allows, and the buyer's actual procurement path. Agree on three questions the series will answer.

Days 8-20. Publish two reviewed explainers, structure them for search and AI citation, and run one unconventional experiment the founders like.

Days 21-30. Track which pieces earn qualified demos. Write an honest report on what worked and kill what did not.

My SIFMA Capitol Hill Challenge research placed second nationally and taught me to separate a compelling story from the evidence under it. Insurance buyers think exactly that way.
