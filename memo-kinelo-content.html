Stale knowledge is a pain every team recognizes in a screenshot.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Kinelo keeps company knowledge current for people and AI, and its marketing should dramatize the moment everyone knows: the confident answer built on last quarter's doc. Recognition is the wedge; the product demo is the relief.

What I noticed

Kinelo is an early-stage AI startup building technology that keeps company knowledge current and available to both people and AI. It is hiring a part-time Marketing & Content Intern, San Francisco or remote in the US, at $25 an hour.

I'd test a before-it-breaks content series: short, specific scenes of stale knowledge costing a team real time, the wrong pricing page quoted to a prospect, the deprecated process followed by a new hire, each ending with the same question answered against a living source. The audience should feel caught, not sold to. Measure signups from teams per story. That is a format hypothesis, not a claim that Kinelo's current content lacks relevance.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Interview five operators about the last time stale knowledge cost them, and collect the exact phrasing they use.

Days 8-20. Turn the three sharpest scenes into short posts and one teardown page, and test them against a feature-first control.

Days 21-30. Double down on the scene that drives signups and build a repeatable weekly format around it.

I ran my own e-commerce store to $5K+ in monthly revenue, and even solo, stale notes cost me real orders; at team scale that pain multiplies. I grew Flywheelr to 100K+ LinkedIn impressions on recognition-first content, and I would love to spend a summer 2027 internship building that muscle for Kinelo.
