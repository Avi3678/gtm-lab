Scout / Growth & sales

Lead with the retail decision, then show its rows.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Scout's traceability is a useful bridge between an impressive analysis and a retail team acting on it.

What I noticed

The homepage connects retailer and SKU-level data with replenishment, promotions and distribution decisions. It also emphasizes tracing metrics to source rows and labels dashboard examples as sample data. That combination suggests a practical story: not simply 'AI found an opportunity,' but which decision changed and how someone can inspect the reasoning.

I'd test a source-led category-review brief around one question, such as whether a promotion produced incremental volume or shifted timing. The example would show the baseline and what the available data cannot establish. My hypothesis is that this helps an operator evaluate the workflow; I do not know Scout's current conversion rates or which retail problem is most urgent for its buyers.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the priority customer segment and recurring decision. Review approved data examples, definitions and permissions. Agree on what a qualified inquiry or useful product action looks like.

Days 8-20. Create one reviewed brief using synthetic data or a fully approved case. Connect the chart to a decision and a source check. Prepare a concise version for outreach without implying unsupported customer results.

Days 21-30. Test one approved channel with the relevant audience. Track qualified conversations and questions about data fit. Improve the explanation before extending the format to another retailer or workflow.

My e-commerce work taught me to look past surface revenue, and the Capitol Hill Challenge taught me to defend an analysis. I'd bring those habits alongside Flywheelr GTM experience.
