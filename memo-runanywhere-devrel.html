Faster inference is proven in benchmarks, adopted through tutorials.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

RunAnywhere makes frontier open models faster and cheaper on-device, and developers believe that claim only when they reproduce it. DevRel content here should be reproducible proof first and storytelling second.

What I noticed

RunAnywhere (YC W26) does inference research at the frontier, making open models faster and cheaper on its Wally stack. It is hiring a Developer Relations & Technical Content Intern, remote, at $750 to $1.5K a month, focused on Android and LLM content.

I'd test a reproduce-it-yourself content cadence: one benchmark a week that a mobile developer can rerun on their own phone in under an hour, with the script, the model, and the numbers published together. Trust compounds when the reader's device confirms the claim. Measure repo clones and reruns per post, not impressions. That is a channel hypothesis, not a claim that RunAnywhere's current content overclaims.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Run the flagship benchmarks on an ordinary phone myself and write down every step where the docs made me stop.

Days 8-20. Publish the first two reproduce-it-yourself posts and seed them where Android and on-device AI developers actually argue.

Days 21-30. Turn the reruns into a living benchmark page the community can add to, and report which content drove real installs.

I built Prism AI and I have shipped content that grew Flywheelr to 100K+ LinkedIn impressions; the technical-audience lesson is that receipts outperform adjectives. A summer 2027 internship where the content is the benchmark is the kind of devrel I want to learn.
