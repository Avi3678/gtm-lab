Roundabout / Sales & GTM

Sell the intersection, one city at a time.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: govtech GTM is won with proof a traffic engineer can defend in a city council meeting. Roundabout's numbers are strong; the job is packaging them for the next city.

What I noticed

Roundabout Technologies (YC F24) builds AI-powered traffic lights that reduce wait times and improve safety, sold to civil and traffic engineers at city, county, and state governments. Its public case study cites 15-70% reductions in red-light running and 10-30% reductions in wait time. The team hired a GTM Sales Intern this past summer and is recruiting sales and GTM interns in San Francisco.

I'd test a city-lookalike playbook: take the published results, identify the twenty cities whose congestion profiles and signal infrastructure most resemble the proven deployments, and write a one-page brief per city in the engineer's vocabulary, referencing their own public congestion data. Measure meetings booked per brief against generic outbound. That is a targeting hypothesis, not a claim that Roundabout's current pipeline is unsegmented.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the deployment story, and how the founders sell. Read the case study data and the public procurement context for two target cities.

Days 8-20. Build the lookalike city list and the first five one-page briefs. Sit in on how the team runs discovery and tighten the brief against real objections.

Days 21-30. Run outbound on the next ten cities, track reply and meeting rates by brief version, and document the segments where the story lands best.

In the SIFMA challenge I defended research to Congressional representatives, an audience that punishes vague claims. Selling to public engineers demands the same standard, and I would bring it.
