Uplane / Marketing strategy

The intern should be the AI engine's best user.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Uplane's intern runs real campaigns with its own AI engine, so the fastest way to be valuable is to become the person who knows the product's edges best.

What I noticed

Uplane is building the AI technology for the marketing agency of the future: ad creation, landing pages, and budget allocation in one engine, and its AI Marketing Strategist Intern uses that engine to plan, run, and scale campaigns for real customers, onsite in San Francisco. Companies waste billions on bad ads and mismatched budgets; Uplane's pitch is that software plus judgment beats headcount.

I'd test a personal benchmark practice: for every customer campaign, keep a private scorecard of what the AI engine proposed, what I changed, and how each version performed. Within a month that scorecard becomes the team's clearest map of where the engine needs a human, which improves both the campaigns and the product. That is a craft hypothesis, not a claim that Uplane's strategists do not already learn from results.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the engine hands-on: build test campaigns, review how strategists judge its output, and study two active customer accounts.

Days 8-20. Run my first campaign end to end with the scorecard habit. Share weekly notes on where the engine's judgment diverged from mine and who was right.

Days 21-30. Present the scorecard findings: the three most reliable engine strengths and the three places human judgment still wins, with campaign data.

I have run marketing for my own business with real money on the line, and I build AI products myself. The seat where AI output meets human judgment is exactly where I want to spend a summer.
