Turing Labs / Community

Scientists trust scientists. Build the room where they talk.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Turing Labs sells to R&D leaders, and R&D people adopt tools their peers vouch for. A real community is a GTM channel, not a nice-to-have.

What I noticed

Turing Labs Inc. builds AI for the R&D of consumer brands like P&G, helping formulators develop better products faster. It is hiring a Community Architect Intern in Palo Alto or San Francisco at $4-8K a month, a title that says the company treats community as infrastructure. For a product sold to scientists and formulators inside large CPG companies, peer credibility is the shortest path to a pilot.

I'd test a practitioner roundtable series: small, off-the-record sessions where formulation and R&D leaders compare how they evaluate AI tools, with Turing hosting rather than pitching. The output is a trusted room and a stream of language for content, what practitioners call things, not what vendors call them. Measure pilot conversations that trace back to attendees. That is a community hypothesis, not a claim that Turing's current outreach lacks peer proof.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the customer base, and existing community efforts. Identify twenty practitioners worth inviting and agree on the roundtable format and rules.

Days 8-20. Host the first small roundtable, capture themes and language with permission, and turn them into two pieces of practitioner-voiced content.

Days 21-30. Recruit the second cohort from the first group's referrals, track pilot conversations sourced, and document the community playbook.

At Berkeley I live inside communities that run on trust and word of mouth. Building a room people want to return to is work I have done as a founder and would enjoy doing for Turing's practitioners.
