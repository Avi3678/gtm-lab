Deep24 / Growth & business experiments

Make the intervention part of the result.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Deep24's business testbeds become more informative when growth experiments record human help as carefully as revenue.

What I noticed

The research page describes businesses as live evaluation environments and says humans remain deeply in the loop. It names decisions, interventions and outcomes as things the team tracks. That gives a small business experiment two jobs: test whether customers want something and make clear which parts of the result came from an agent versus a person.

I'd test a narrowly scoped acquisition experiment with an intervention log, a spending limit and a stopping rule agreed in advance. The useful output could be a failed hypothesis if the record explains why it failed. This is an experiment-design hypothesis, not a claim that Deep24 currently misattributes outcomes or that any agent can run a business without oversight.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the team's active testbeds, approved channels and research questions. Choose one low-risk workflow and agree on the budget, authority boundaries and success or stop criteria before taking action.

Days 8-20. Prepare a small experiment with traceable inputs and a simple decision log. Record human corrections, blocked actions and time spent. Use approved data and never make customer or financial commitments outside scope.

Days 21-30. Review customer response, costs and interventions together. Separate a repeatable result from a one-off outcome. Recommend continue, revise or stop based on the pre-agreed rule, not a persuasive story.

Running an e-commerce venture to $5K+ MRR taught me that real customers are a better test than a polished plan. I'd bring that operating mindset while learning Deep24's research methods.
