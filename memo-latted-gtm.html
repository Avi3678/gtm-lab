Go-to-market

AI video ads are bought on the numbers, sold on the taste.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Latted's GTM intern sits between two buyers who want opposite things: performance marketers who want volume and variation, and brand teams who want taste. The job is to sell one engine to both without sounding generic to either.

What I noticed

Latted is an all-in-one AI content engine for organic video and paid ads: generate, edit, and iterate on professional video projects that take more than a single generation. It is hiring a GTM Intern in San Francisco or remote, at $15 to $35 an hour, working directly with the founder.

I'd test a split-proof content motion: one series built for performance marketers, real ad variants with real iteration counts, and one built for brand leads, one hero video refined through the edit graph end to end. The same product, two kinds of evidence. Measure demo requests per series, not views. That is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim that Latted's current outreach blends the two audiences.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Ship one ad variant set and one hero edit with the product myself, and document the iteration count each took. You cannot sell an edit graph you have not felt.

Days 8-20. Build the two tracks: twenty performance accounts and twenty brand accounts, each with an opening asset made with Latted for their actual product. Track replies separately from day one.

Days 21-30. Write up which track converts and why, and turn the winning openings into a repeatable kit the founder can reuse without me.

I grew Flywheelr to 100K+ LinkedIn impressions, and the lesson that transferred was that taste content and performance content are different jobs. Latted is the first tool I have seen that genuinely tries to serve both, and I would love to learn go-to-market where that tension is the work.
