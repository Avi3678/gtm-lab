Sixtyfour / Growth marketing

Teach the evidence trail, not just the answer.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Sixtyfour's growth story can start with how a finding becomes reviewable, rather than how much information an agent can find.

What I noticed

The homepage describes research beginning with an identifier, crossing public records and other sources, and returning findings with supporting evidence. It also separates uses including fraud, workforce risk and financial crime. Those buyers may all need research, but they do not have the same review process or decision threshold.

I'd test one clearly labeled synthetic investigation showing a claim, its supporting source and the point where a human reviewer should ask another question. That could make the product's evidence model tangible without using a real person's sensitive information. It is a messaging hypothesis, not a claim that Sixtyfour's current content fails or that a research result alone proves wrongdoing.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn one priority audience and its review workflow. Ask the team which evidence standards and privacy constraints should govern any public example. Review existing case studies before choosing a gap.

Days 8-20. Build a source-led educational brief using approved synthetic entities. Distinguish a match, a possible relationship and a verified conclusion. Get product and appropriate policy review.

Days 21-30. Test distribution in one approved channel. Track qualified conversations and whether readers understand the evidence workflow. Collect objections before proposing another audience or a larger campaign.

My Capitol Hill Challenge work required research I could explain under questioning. I'd bring that habit, along with content production experience from Flywheelr, to a team-defined growth priority.
