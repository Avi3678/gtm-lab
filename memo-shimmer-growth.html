Shimmer / Social & growth

No productivity-bro content for an ADHD audience.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Shimmer's members can smell generic hustle content instantly, and the growth channel that fits an ADHD coaching brand is honest, member-shaped storytelling.

What I noticed

Shimmer offers 1:1 ADHD coaching at five to ten times lower cost than alternatives, and it is hiring a Social Media Manager & Growth Hacker intern, remote in the US at $20-30 an hour. The audience is people who have spent years being told to try harder, buy another planner, or just focus. Content that sounds like every other productivity account will actively damage the brand.

I'd test a member-language content series: real phrases and moments from the ADHD community, body doubling, doom piles, time blindness, turned into short videos that name the experience before they name the product. The test is saves and shares within ADHD communities, not broad reach. That is a voice hypothesis, not a claim that Shimmer's current content misses its audience.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the coaching model, and the community's vocabulary. Review existing content and flag anything that reads as generic productivity advice.

Days 8-20. Produce the first member-language series: three short videos naming one real ADHD experience each, ending with how coaching addresses it. Publish and log saves and shares.

Days 21-30. Compare performance against the content baseline, identify the two experiences that resonated most, and build the next two weeks around them.

I like brands that earn attention by being specific about who they are for. Building my own products taught me that the sharper the audience picture, the easier every content decision gets.
