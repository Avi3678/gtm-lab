Clera / Growth & marketing

Market the intro, not the platform.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Clera's most credible growth content is the moment of introduction itself: who got introduced, why it fit, and what happened next.

What I noticed

Clera replaces job applications with warm introductions: a candidate describes what they want over WhatsApp or iMessage, and Clera pitches them directly to hiring managers at 900+ startups. The numbers are specific: 250K+ candidates, eight days from signup to first interview, and companies pay only on a successful hire. The growth role spans outbound, paid, content, SEO, partnerships and events, with an emphasis on measurable outcomes.

I'd test a weekly intro-story format with consent from both sides: one introduction, the fit reason in a single sentence, and the outcome thirty days later. It markets both sides of the marketplace in the same artifact, and it makes the eight-day claim tangible rather than a statistic. Judge it on reply rates and interview conversion, not reach. That is a content hypothesis, not a critique of Clera's current channels.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the matching flow, the privacy rules for candidate stories, and which channels produce interviews today. Agree on the story format and its consent steps.

Days 8-20. Publish two reviewed intro stories, adapt them for candidate-side and founder-side audiences, and track responses per audience.

Days 21-30. Run one channel experiment, such as a founder-dinner recap or a campus partnership post. Report which story elements predicted interviews.

At Flywheelr I built outreach that felt individual at scale, which is the same craft as a good intro. I would bring that while learning marketplace growth from a team that includes a Berkeley alum.
