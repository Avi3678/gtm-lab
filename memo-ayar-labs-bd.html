Ayar Labs / Business development

Sell the deployment answer, not only the optical link.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: the Wiwynn partnership creates a useful sales conversation around operating a rack, not just the performance of an optical component.

What I noticed

In its May 27 rack-scale article, Ayar explains cooling, fiber routing, field serviceability and manufacturable integration alongside optical connectivity. The article says potential customers need proof in real operating conditions. That makes the deployment questions part of the buying decision, not details to leave until after an introduction.

I'd test buyer-specific evidence briefs: a hyperscaler operator, ASIC designer and rack integrator may read the same announcement but need different proof before an evaluation. That's a hypothesis, not a diagnosis of Ayar's pipeline.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the evaluation path from sales and technical teams; map buyer roles to the deployment questions they own.

Days 8-20. Draft one technically reviewed evidence brief for a priority buyer role. Map a small set of accounts, separating known facts from fit hypotheses.

Days 21-30. Support a team-approved outreach test. Log qualified technical conversations, objections and agreed evaluation next steps, not announcement engagement.

I'd bring research discipline from the Capitol Hill Challenge and follow-up experience from Flywheelr, then work on the team's actual priority.
