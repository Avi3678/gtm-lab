Simular / Business operations & growth

Choose the first workflow by its failure cost.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Simular's individual and business audiences make workflow selection an important part of the growth story.

What I noticed

The homepage distinguishes Sai for multitaskers, Simular for Business for organizations with repetitive computer tasks, and developer-facing frameworks and API access. Those audiences can share an interest in computer automation while having very different tolerances for mistakes. A convenient personal task and a business process with customer or financial effects need different evaluation questions.

I'd test an opportunity brief that pairs potential time savings with reversibility, permissions and the cost of an incorrect action. The first candidate would be a bounded workflow the team considers suitable, not whichever task sounds most impressive. My hypothesis is that this makes an evaluation easier to scope. The site does not establish which use cases convert or how any particular deployment performs.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the priority customer segment and how the team evaluates workflow fit. Ask what data, permissions and human checkpoints a pilot needs. Review approved examples before proposing a use case.

Days 8-20. Map one low-risk workflow with inputs, expected output, exception handling and an acceptance check. Separate estimated capacity from measured savings. Get product review before using the brief in a customer conversation.

Days 21-30. Support an approved pilot-discovery effort or analyze an approved existing case. Record completion, corrections and recovery work together. Recommend the next test based on evidence rather than a broad automation promise.

Building Prism AI has taught me to take failure modes seriously. I'd bring that technical curiosity and Flywheelr GTM experience while learning the team's actual operating priorities.
