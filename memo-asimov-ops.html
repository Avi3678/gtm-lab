Asimov / Strategic operations

The humanoid race is a data race.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: every humanoid lab is starved for real-world movement data, so Asimov's operations are the product. The intern's leverage is making collection throughput visible and improvable.

What I noticed

Asimov collects real-world human movement data for humanoid robots, and it is hiring a Strategic Operations Intern in Berkeley, or remote in the US, at $20-50 an hour. As humanoid labs race to generalize manipulation and locomotion, the bottleneck is shifting from models to diverse, high-quality physical data, exactly what Asimov sells. A Berkeley role at a YC company in this space is rare.

I'd test an operations dashboard project: one page that tracks collection sessions, hours of usable data, annotation pass rates, and delivery against customer commitments. If throughput is the product, then measuring it like a product metric gives the founders leverage in every customer and investor conversation. That is an ops hypothesis, not a claim that Asimov's current tracking is weak.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the collection pipeline end to end: how sessions are sourced, run, annotated, and delivered. Shadow the ops workflow before proposing anything.

Days 8-20. Build the first version of the throughput dashboard from existing data. Review it weekly with the team and fix what it gets wrong.

Days 21-30. Use the dashboard to find the single largest bottleneck, quantify it, and run one experiment to relieve it.

I am a Berkeley student who has run operations for my own business, where weekly numbers decided what happened next. Working on humanoid data ops fifteen minutes from campus would be a privilege.
