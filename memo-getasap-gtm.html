GETASAP / Go-to-market

Qualify the operating lane before the sales pitch.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: GETASAP's multiple logistics offerings make operational fit a useful first qualification question.

What I noticed

The homepage separates same-day produce delivery, AI-native freight brokerage, demand intelligence and scheduling/dispatch. A retailer buying produce and a business looking for freight capacity may see the same site but need different conversations. The delivery page language also makes order timing part of the proposition, not just an administrative detail.

I'd test a discovery brief that identifies which service a prospect actually needs before ranking the account. For a produce buyer, questions might include geography, ordering cadence and receiving constraints; the operations team would define the valid boundaries. This is a qualification hypothesis, not a claim that GETASAP currently pursues unsuitable accounts. I would not treat sample dashboard figures as customer outcomes.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn current service coverage, customer segments and the handoff between sales and operations. Confirm which promises a rep can make and which require an operational check.

Days 8-20. Research a small team-approved account set using public information. Label inferred fit clearly. Draft service-specific discovery questions and a concise, reviewed introduction, without committing capacity or price.

Days 21-30. Support an approved outreach test. Track qualified conversations, operational fit and agreed next steps. Review why prospects do not fit before expanding the list or changing the message.

My Flywheelr AI-SDR work taught me to connect prospect research with follow-up. I'd bring that discipline and learn the practical constraints of produce logistics before proposing scale.
