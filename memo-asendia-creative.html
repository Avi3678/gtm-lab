Asendia AI / Creative marketing

Show what the recruiter gets back.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Asendia's recruiter-assistance positioning is most concrete at the handoff back into the recruiting workflow.

What I noticed

The homepage says 'Don't replace your recruiters' and shows Sarah moving through sourcing, a phone screen and follow-ups. It also says shortlists, transcripts and scores sync into an ATS. That gives creative content a more specific story than an AI voice making calls: what information can a recruiter review, and what decision does that help them make?

I'd test a short, clearly synthetic workflow story ending with the recruiter's review, not with an animated conversation alone. It would distinguish screening support from a hiring decision and avoid unsupported claims about candidate quality or fairness. My hypothesis is that a visible handoff makes the value easier to understand; the public site does not establish which creative currently generates qualified interest.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the priority agency segment and the team's approved product and hiring-related claims. Review existing creative and asset rights. Ask which buyer questions recur around the handoff.

Days 8-20. Draft two hooks for one reviewed workflow: time spent chasing responses versus clarity at shortlist review. Use synthetic candidates and accurate interface material. Get product and appropriate policy review before release.

Days 21-30. Run a small approved creative test. Compare qualified agency inquiries and the questions they ask, not views alone. Use feedback to improve the next story rather than invent stronger results.

At Flywheelr I worked on content workflows behind 100K+ LinkedIn impressions. I'd bring fast production and a conversion mindset while learning the staffing context from Asendia's team.
