Kinro / Content & brand

Film the broker's day, not the insurance ad.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: nobody shares insurance content, but everyone shares a good story about a small-business owner. Kinro's content should live in the field.

What I noticed

Kinro is building the AI-native insurance brokerage for America's small businesses, and its Content & Brand Intern posting reads like a creator job, not a marketing internship: film, edit, and appear in videos, spend time in the field with small-business owners, and prove that even general liability can hold attention. The role is in person in San Francisco, pays $4-10K a month, and explicitly includes building the founders' public profiles through podcasts and media.

I'd test a recurring field series: one small-business owner per week, filmed at their shop, telling the story of the moment insurance actually mattered to them, with Kinro appearing only as the broker who handled it. The test is whether owner-first storytelling earns shares from other owners, which is the exact audience Kinro sells to. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim that product-led content cannot work.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the customer segments, and the founders' voices. Sit with the team to pick the first three owner stories and handle consent and logistics.

Days 8-20. Film and edit the first two field episodes, cut them into short clips per platform, and publish. Track completion rate and shares among owner accounts.

Days 21-30. Pitch two podcasts or creators for a founder appearance using the field footage as proof. Report which story format traveled furthest and lock the weekly cadence.

Running my own small business on weekly numbers taught me how owners actually talk about risk and paperwork. I would be comfortable on camera and in their shops, and I would bring that fluency to Kinro's field content.
