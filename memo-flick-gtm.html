Flick / Go-to-market

Distribute the making-of, not only the film.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: the behind-the-scenes workflow is Flick's most portable asset. It teaches, and it travels further than the finished film.

What I noticed

Flick is an AI-native filmmaking platform: script, storyboard, shots and edit in one workspace, with a library of over a million classical film scenes for reference. Four films made with Flick have collected 20+ international festival awards, and the team just launched Flick TV as a home for original AI films, alongside a creator residency producing shorts every week. The GTM role covers campaigns, distribution across TikTok, Instagram, Discord and communities, and turning product features into narratives.

I'd test packaging one award-recognized film as a public workflow breakdown: the references used, the shot decisions, the edit choices, published as a reusable template inside the community. The film proves quality; the breakdown creates users, because a creator can follow it themselves. Compare template saves and signups against trailer-style posts. That is a distribution hypothesis, not a judgment on the current content mix.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Watch the films, read the docs, join the Discord. Learn which creators and festivals drive the most qualified interest today, and agree on one breakdown format with the founders.

Days 8-20. Produce one reviewed workflow breakdown with a residency creator, cut it into short-form pieces per platform, and publish with a clear path to try the template.

Days 21-30. Compare performance against the existing announcement posts. Collect the questions new creators ask, then decide with the team whether the format becomes a series.

At Flywheelr I ran personalized content workflows, and I learned that tailored material beats volume when the audience is discerning. Filmmakers are exactly that audience.
