CatchBack Cards / Growth

Make the verifiable odds the hook.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: in a category with a trust problem, the cryptographic verification is a stronger hook than the chase card.

What I noticed

CatchBack lets collectors build custom mystery packs with personalized chases and cryptographically trusted odds, rip them digitally, get physical cards shipped, and receive buyback offers straight to Venmo or PayPal. Mystery packs have a reputation problem: buyers reasonably assume the house wins quietly. The growth role is execution-heavy: short-form video for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, creator coordination, and fast growth experiments.

I'd test content that opens on the skeptic's question (how do I know the odds are real?) and shows the verification working on an actual pack before showing the rip. Trust first, thrill second. Most pack content leads with the pull; leading with the proof may filter in buyers who currently scroll past the whole category. Compare watch-through and first-pack conversion against chase-first edits. That is a creative hypothesis, not a claim about current performance.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the collector communities that matter, and the claims the team is comfortable making on camera. Agree on one trust-first format and one conversion metric.

Days 8-20. Cut two reviewed videos in the format from real pack footage, brief one creator on the same angle, and track what each earns.

Days 21-30. Run one experiment on the buyback moment as a second hook. Review numbers with the founders and keep only what converts.

Running an e-commerce business to $5K+ in monthly recurring revenue taught me that conversion starts with believing the seller. I would bring that lens while learning the collectibles market from the team.
