Make the rack story useful to a specific reader.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: the most useful content in Ayar's Wiwynn announcement is the part about making optics work in a production rack.

What I noticed

Ayar's May 27 article explains cooling, fiber routing, serviceability and manufacturing alongside optical performance. It explicitly says customers need proof in operating conditions. That gives marketing a concrete teaching job: show what has to be true for deployment, rather than repeat a bigger-bandwidth headline.

I'd test one source-led content series around deployment questions. A short visual could explain fiber management; a longer piece could explain what serviceability means to an operator. The formats would share reviewed technical evidence, but each would answer one reader's question. This is a content hypothesis, not evidence that Ayar's current messaging is missing those readers.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn which questions sales and engineering answer repeatedly. Agree on one audience and one desired next action. Audit existing material before proposing anything new.

Days 8-20. Turn one approved technical explanation into a visual post, a short article and a sales follow-up asset. Keep a source sheet and get technical review before publishing.

Days 21-30. Run a team-approved distribution test. Compare qualified replies, useful follow-up questions and use by sales, not impressions alone. Document what the next version should change.

At Flywheelr I built content workflows behind 100K+ LinkedIn impressions. I'd bring that production experience while learning the technical details from people who know them.
