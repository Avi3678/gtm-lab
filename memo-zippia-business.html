A job platform grows where the search is most desperate.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Zippia helps people find jobs and career paths, and a business intern's sharpest lens is segment desperation: the worker whose industry is shrinking, the new grad with a vague major, the city losing employers. Growth follows the people who need the answer most.

What I noticed

Zippia is a San Mateo career platform used by millions of job seekers, and it is hiring a Business Intern through the NextView job network. Career platforms win by owning the searches nobody else serves well, and the business work is finding which underserved segment converts to loyalty.

I'd test a segment-deep-dive project: pick two job-seeker segments with high urgency and low satisfaction, map their search behavior and where they drop off, and turn the findings into a partnership or content motion per segment. Measure activation per segment, not raw traffic. That is a research hypothesis, not a claim that Zippia's current segments are wrong.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Analyze the funnel data available and pick the two highest-stakes segments to study.

Days 8-20. Run the deep dives: interviews, search data, drop-off points, and one test motion per segment.

Days 21-30. Present which segment deserves investment and the evidence behind the recommendation.

I ran my own e-commerce store to $5K+ in monthly revenue and placed 2nd nationally in the SIFMA competition; both rewarded segment-level honesty over aggregate averages. A summer 2027 internship doing that analysis at a career platform is exactly the business work I want to learn.
