Myto / GTM & business development

Factories buy from people who show up.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: manufacturing buyers trust presence, so Myto's GTM intern should treat conferences, plant visits, and founder-led meetings as the core channel, with outbound as the follow-through.

What I noticed

Myto builds AI that learns from the factory environment and turns that knowledge into better decisions on the floor. The team brings experience from Stanford, BCG, Porter, and Mercedes-Benz, the founders previously built a unicorn, and the company is well funded with active design partners and a waitlist of manufacturers. Its GTM & Business Development Intern works directly with the founders on sales, customer conversations, partnerships, events, and strategy, in Palo Alto or remote, and the posting is explicit that exceptional interns may get a full-time offer.

I'd test an event-anchored account motion: for each manufacturing conference or trade gathering the founders attend, build the target account list in advance, research each plant's public signals, expansions, hiring, downtime incidents, and book meetings around the event rather than cold. Measure meetings per event against cold outbound. That is a channel hypothesis, not a claim that Myto's current pipeline is underworked.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the design-partner story, and the manufacturing segments in the waitlist. Shadow how the founders open customer conversations.

Days 8-20. Build the first event-anchored account list with one-page briefs per account. Support live customer meetings and log the objections heard.

Days 21-30. Run the follow-through motion after one event, track meeting-to-pilot conversion, and document the playbook for the next one.

Selling to operators who have seen every pitch is a challenge I want early. My competition experience defending research to skeptical executives is training for exactly these rooms.
