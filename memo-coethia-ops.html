Trust in health content is an operations problem.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Coethia's product is credibility, and credibility at scale is built by operations - the clinician review pipeline, the creator onboarding, the standards checklist - not by any single piece of content.

What I noticed

Coethia, founded by CEO Kate Mayberg (Stanford MS, ex-Google), is building a new model for how health information is created, distributed, and trusted: partnering with clinicians, students, and creators to produce credible health content and the systems behind it. It is hiring a paid, part-time Operations Intern, remote within the US, posted about two weeks ago, as an early team hiring across functions.

I'd test building the trust pipeline as an explicit system: a documented review workflow from draft to clinician sign-off to publication, an onboarding kit for new creators that encodes the standards, and a simple dashboard of cycle times and reviewer load. For a company whose differentiator is evidence-based content, the operating system behind that claim is what lets it scale without diluting. That is an operations hypothesis, not a claim that Coethia's current process is informal.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Shadow the current content and review process end to end, and write down every step, owner, and bottleneck as it actually works today.

Days 8-20. Draft the review pipeline and creator onboarding kit with the team, pilot them on the next batch of content, and measure cycle time.

Days 21-30. Refine the system from the pilot, build the tracking dashboard, and document the playbook so it runs without me.

Health misinformation spreads partly because credible producers are slower than everyone else - and speed is an operations problem. Building the system that lets trustworthy content move faster without cutting corners is exactly the kind of quiet, high-leverage work I want to learn early.
