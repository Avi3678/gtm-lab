Scalar Field / Community, social & creator growth

Build a community around testing the idea.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Scalar Field's idea-to-backtest workflow gives its community a better recurring activity than sharing market predictions.

What I noticed

The role description connects natural-language trading agents with research, backtesting and execution, and explicitly calls for community activities and credible, non-spammy participation. That creates a useful distinction for social growth: a lively market conversation is not necessarily a useful product-learning experience, and a backtest is not evidence of future returns.

I'd test a moderated educational challenge where members explain a hypothesis, its assumptions and how it could fail, using historical or paper examples only. The objective would be learning the workflow, not encouraging live trades or promoting a winning strategy. My hypothesis is that this could build useful repeat participation; I do not know Scalar Field's current community retention or activation data.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn community rules, the priority user and approved financial-language boundaries. Review current conversations and ask what a useful first product action looks like. Agree on escalation and moderation responsibilities.

Days 8-20. Draft one reviewed challenge with a simple rubric, risk caveats and a clear feedback loop. Use synthetic or historical examples and explain backtest limits. Coordinate with moderators before any distribution.

Days 21-30. Run a team-approved session or content test. Measure relevant participation, completion and return to the next learning activity, not member count alone. Summarize product questions without giving investment advice.

My Capitol Hill Challenge experience taught me to explain research rather than just announce a result. I'd combine that discipline with Flywheelr content experience while learning from the community.
