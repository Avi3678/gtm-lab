GTM engineering

An AI growth marketer should grow itself first.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Yobo is an AI growth marketer businesses hire, and the sharpest proof a GTM engineer intern can build is Yobo running Yobo: the company's own pipeline built, run, and measured by the product. That is the demo, the case study, and the sales asset in one.

What I noticed

Yobo is building an AI growth marketer that businesses hire to run their growth, and it is hiring a GTM Engineer Intern, part-time with performance bonus, in Berkeley and the Bay Area. Selling growth software to operators who have been burned by agencies means every claim needs a receipt.

I'd test a self-hosting proof motion: instrument Yobo's own acquisition end to end, publish the weekly numbers honestly, what the AI ran, what a human overrode, what it cost per meeting, and turn that transparency into the sales narrative. Buyers of growth tooling are the most skeptical audience there is, and open numbers are the only thing that moves them. That is a proof hypothesis, not a claim that Yobo's current motion lacks receipts.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Map the current pipeline honestly: where leads come from, what converts, what the AI touches today.

Days 8-20. Run one full self-hosted growth cycle and document it as a public build log with real numbers.

Days 21-30. Turn the log into the sales narrative and a repeatable experiment cadence the team can continue.

I built Prism AI and ran my own e-commerce store's growth to $5K+ in monthly revenue, so working on both sides of the AI-growth line is familiar. A Berkeley-based summer 2027 internship doing proof-first GTM engineering is exactly where I want to be.
