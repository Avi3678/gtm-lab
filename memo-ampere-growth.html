Ampere / Sales & growth

Reindustrialization is a story operations leaders fact-check.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: manufacturing buyers have seen a decade of AI demos. Ampere's growth motion should lead with deployed proof on real factory floors, and the intern can make that proof findable.

What I noticed

Ampere builds physical AI for American manufacturing and is backed by Halcyon Venture Partners, which wrote publicly about investing in the reindustrialization thesis. The company runs recurring internship programs, a summer intern in 2025 focused on go-to-market strategy, a fall intern on the sales and marketing team in 2026, and it is hiring a Sales & Growth Intern in San Francisco now.

I'd test a proof-map project: collect every public deployment detail, customer quote, and measurable outcome Ampere can share, then organize it by the objections operations leaders actually raise, downtime, integration with existing lines, and workforce trust. Sales conversations start from the objection, not the pitch deck. Measure how often proof assets get used in live deals. That is an enablement hypothesis, not a claim that Ampere's current materials lack proof.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the deployments, and the sales motion. Interview the team on the five objections that come up most and audit existing proof assets.

Days 8-20. Build the proof map, draft the missing one-pagers for the top two objections, and support live outreach with matched materials.

Days 21-30. Track asset usage and response quality in active conversations, refine the map, and leave the team a maintained objection-to-proof library.

I like markets where trust is earned with evidence. Defending quantitative work to skeptical expert audiences in competitions is the closest thing I have done to selling into operations leaders.
