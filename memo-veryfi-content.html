Veryfi / Content marketing

Write for the developer who got voluntold.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Veryfi's real reader is often a developer told to 'just add receipt scanning' by Friday. Content that respects that deadline wins the account.

What I noticed

Veryfi provides document data extraction APIs: receipts, invoices, W-2s and more, turned into structured data in seconds, and it sells to product teams building expense, loyalty, and fintech workflows. The Content Marketing Intern role sits in San Mateo at $24-28 an hour, supporting the content engine for a technical product with a business buyer watching over it.

I'd test a build-log series aimed at that voluntold developer: short, honest posts that start from a real integration task, like parsing crumpled receipts or matching line items, and show the working path in minutes. Each post ends with the JSON, because developers trust output, not adjectives. Measure signups from post to API key created. That is a content hypothesis, not a claim that Veryfi's current docs or blog miss this reader.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the API surface, the customer verticals, and the voice of existing content. Get a test key and run real documents through the API to feel the workflow.

Days 8-20. Draft the first two build-log posts with real output screenshots, get engineering review, and publish through the normal channel. Seed them where developers ask for receipt OCR help.

Days 21-30. Compare signup and key-creation attribution on the series against the blog baseline, then propose the next three tasks in the series.

I have shipped my own product and presented quantitative work to financial executives. Writing plainly about technical output for a business audience is exactly the middle I like working in.
