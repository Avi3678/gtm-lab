Helium / Social media & growth

Show the experiment behind the paywall.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Helium's strongest educational content could explain how to judge an experiment, not only how to design a paywall.

What I noticed

The homepage combines a paywall builder with subscription analytics, experimentation and segmentation. It also describes app-to-web subscription flows and SDK integration. Those are connected decisions: an attractive screen is only one part of understanding who saw it, what they did and what happened after subscribing.

I'd test a public, synthetic experiment breakdown that begins with a business question and ends with a cautious decision. It would keep conversion separate from longer-term subscriber quality and make the sample limitations visible. That's a content hypothesis, not an assertion that Helium customers measure the wrong things or that a specific design will lift revenue.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the team's target app segment and approved customer evidence. Agree on one educational question and a meaningful product action. Review current content before choosing what to add.

Days 8-20. Create one reviewed experiment story using synthetic numbers or an approved customer case. Show the audience, comparison, metric and decision rule. Turn it into a short visual post and a deeper guide.

Days 21-30. Test one approved distribution channel. Compare qualified developer interest and the agreed activation action, then collect questions about implementation and measurement. Expand only if the content helps the intended audience.

Running an e-commerce business to $5K+ MRR taught me to connect creative work with purchase behavior. I'd bring that perspective and Flywheelr content experience while learning mobile subscription economics from the team.
