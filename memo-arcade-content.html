Arcade / Social media & content

One creation, two different reasons to care.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Arcade's consumer and brand audiences need different stories even when they see the same physical product.

What I noticed

The internship posting explicitly says Arcade serves both consumers and e-commerce brands, connecting product creation with the moment a customer decides to buy. It also calls for content across Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest and other channels. That suggests an interesting creative distinction: the person imagining an object and the brand evaluating a commercial workflow are not necessarily asking the same question.

I'd test two stories built from one approved creation. The consumer version would show the idea, design choices and result; the brand version would explain the reviewed workflow and what remains a human decision. This is an audience hypothesis, not evidence that Arcade's current content mixes the two or that any particular format will convert.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn which audience and product area are the priority. Review existing creative, rights to assets and the technical claims a post can make. Agree on one meaningful next action.

Days 8-20. Create a small storyboard set from one approved product example. Separate concept imagery from a manufactured result and get design/product review. Prepare channel-specific edits rather than reposting the same clip everywhere.

Days 21-30. Run a small approved distribution test. Compare relevant engagement and the agreed creation or inquiry action by audience. Record what viewers misunderstand and change the next creative brief.

I grew an e-commerce business to $5K+ MRR and worked on content workflows at Flywheelr. I'd bring that connection between creative work and buying behavior while learning Arcade's product.
