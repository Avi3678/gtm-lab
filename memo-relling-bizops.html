Relling / Business operations

A robot that runs from day one needs operations that do too.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Relling's promise is turnkey automation, and turnkey is an operations claim, so the bizops work behind deployments is as much the product as the workcell itself.

What I noticed

Relling, founded by Anya Singh and Jai Relan, builds turnkey robotic workcells for American manufacturers: configured to a customer's part, running from day one, designed so existing staff stay safe and employed. It posted a Business Operations Intern role in San Francisco about two weeks ago, signaling that the company is investing in the operating layer behind its deployments, not just the hardware.

I'd test a deployment-metrics system: define the handful of numbers that prove turnkey is real, install-to-running time, first-pass yield, downtime per week, and build the internal dashboard and customer-facing report around them. For a company whose pitch is reliability, operational proof is a sales asset. That is an operations hypothesis, not a claim that Relling lacks deployment discipline.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the deployment process end-to-end, from signed deal to running workcell, and write down where time and information are lost today.

Days 8-20. Draft the turnkey scorecard with the team, collect baseline numbers from current deployments, and build the first live dashboard.

Days 21-30. Turn the scorecard into a customer-facing reliability report, and document the weekly operating cadence the team can keep running after I leave.

Manufacturing is a domain where credibility comes from numbers, not narrative, and that suits how I work. Building the reporting that proves a hard promise is kept is the kind of operations project I want on my record.
