Scope / Growth & marketing

Students recruit students better than ads do.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: a coursework tool spreads through campuses by trust, so Scope's growth motion should look like a campus ambassador engine, not an ad account.

What I noticed

Scope is building the place where students do their coursework: reading, writing, coding, and studying, all inside the course itself. Its careers page lists a Growth & Marketing Intern who grows Scope through campus partnerships, campaigns, and outreach to students, and it says interns work on real parts of the product from day one. The founder is active in the Berkeley ecosystem, including Cal Hacks.

I'd test a campus-first growth loop: recruit a small group of students at Berkeley and two comparable campuses, give them a simple kit, study-group demos, a referral link, a feedback channel, and measure signups per campus per week. Campus density matters more than total signups for a collaboration-shaped product. That is a channel hypothesis, not a claim that Scope's current outreach is ad-driven.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product inside out as a student user: run my own coursework through it and note where it earns a recommendation. Map existing campus outreach.

Days 8-20. Launch the Berkeley pilot: recruit five students, run two study-group demos, and track signups and activation per session.

Days 21-30. Report what converted and what students asked for, write the campus playbook, and spec the second campus launch.

I am the target user: a Berkeley student who does coursework daily and knows exactly where students discover tools. Running campus growth for a product I would actually use is the most honest marketing I can do.
