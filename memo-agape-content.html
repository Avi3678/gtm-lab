Agapé / Social & content

Wholesome scales when it is specific.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: relationship content on social is crowded with generic advice, so Agapé's edge is the specific question that makes a couple pause and talk.

What I noticed

Agapé helps couples feel close even when apart through daily meaningful questions, and it is hiring a Social Media Marketing Intern as a content creator, remote in the US at $17-25 an hour. The product is a prompt engine; the marketing should behave like one. The best-performing relationship content is not tips, it is the question viewers immediately want to ask their partner.

I'd test a question-first content series: each post leads with one genuinely good question, the kind that starts a real conversation, and the product appears only as the source of a new one every day. The metric is shares to a partner, the most natural distribution a couples app can have. That is a format hypothesis, not a claim that Agapé's current content is generic.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the app, the question library, and which content has performed. Study the comments for the questions audiences already respond to.

Days 8-20. Create the first question-first series across TikTok and Instagram. Track shares and saves per question type.

Days 21-30. Identify the three question categories that travel best, build the next two weeks around them, and document the sourcing process for new questions.

I make things and then have to get strangers to care about them. Agapé's challenge, earning a share to a partner, is the most honest version of that work I can imagine.
