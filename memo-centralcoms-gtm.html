CentralComs / Go-to-market

Every missed call is a competitor's booking.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: home-services owners understand exactly one metric instantly: jobs lost to missed calls. CentralComs' GTM should quantify that loss per prospect before pitching anything.

What I noticed

CentralComs (YC X2026), a Berkeley-founded company, builds AI agents for home-services companies: answering every call, routing jobs to the right technician, and keeping calendars full, working inside ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, and Jobber. The pitch writes itself: when the phone rings during a job and nobody picks up, that lead goes to a competitor, and idle techs are pure margin loss.

I'd test a missed-call audit as the opening move: for a target list of local HVAC, plumbing, and electrical companies, estimate the revenue at stake from public review patterns and call-volume proxies, then open with the one number that hurts. The audit is the demo. Measure booking rate of audit-first outreach against feature-first. That is a messaging hypothesis, not a claim that CentralComs' current pitch leads with features.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn the product, the ServiceTitan and Housecall Pro integrations, and how the founders sell today. Listen to real handled calls if available.

Days 8-20. Build the first audit-based prospect list for one metro and run the outreach. Sit in on every demo booked.

Days 21-30. Compare booking rates by opening angle, refine the audit template, and document the motion so the team can rerun it in the next metro.

Berkeley-founded and selling to local businesses: this is close to home in both senses. I ran a small business myself, and I know how owners think about a missed lead.
