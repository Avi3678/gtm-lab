Growth engineering

Selling autonomous companies means showing one small company running.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Naive is building the infrastructure for AI to run real businesses end to end, which is a claim nobody believes until they watch it happen. Growth here is not marketing the vision; it is narrowing the vision to one business a prospect can watch run itself.

What I noticed

Naive (YC S25) is an AI research lab building autonomous companies: the systems, environments, and agent infrastructure for AI to operate real businesses end to end. It is hiring a Growth Engineer Intern in Mountain View or remote in the US, at $6K to $10K a month.

I'd test a smallest-real-business program: pick one tiny, legible operation, a newsletter ad desk, a dropshipping storefront, and run it on Naive's stack in public, with a weekly scoreboard of decisions made without a human. Skeptics do not argue with a running P&L. Measure qualified founder and investor conversations per weekly update. That is a proof hypothesis, not a claim that Naive's current demos lack ambition.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Map the thinnest end-to-end business the stack can run today, honestly, including where a human still has to step in.

Days 8-20. Stand up the public scoreboard and the weekly build log, and recruit a small group of founders to follow along and poke at it.

Days 21-30. Package the first month into the story the founders can tell investors and design partners: what ran, what broke, what is next.

I built Prism AI and ran my own e-commerce store to $5K+ in monthly revenue, so I have been the small operator whose week this infrastructure wants to absorb. A summer 2027 internship where growth means proving autonomy in public is exactly the work I want to learn.
