Social media

Utility tools go viral when the output is the ad.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

Pixoate turns time-consuming work into simple clicks, counting stock from a photo, receipts into spreadsheets, rooms staged, and its social content should be the output itself: watch the click, get the result. The product demo is the content.

What I noticed

Pixoate builds AI photo, video, PDF, OCR, and counting tools for business, and it is hiring a Social Media Intern, remote in the US. Utility AI wins attention with before-and-after proof: a messy receipt becoming a clean spreadsheet is worth more than any caption.

I'd test a raw-output content series: real inputs from real work scenarios, filmed once, posted with zero polish except the result, each series built around one profession's ugliest task. Measure saves and trial starts per post. That is a format hypothesis, not a claim that Pixoate's current channels over-produce.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Use each tool on realistic messy inputs and collect the transformations that make people stop scrolling.

Days 8-20. Publish the first raw-output series across two channels and track which professions respond.

Days 21-30. Double down on the best-performing task category and build a weekly repeatable format.

I grew Flywheelr to 100K+ LinkedIn impressions by showing real work instead of talking about it. A summer 2027 internship turning product output into distribution is exactly the muscle I want to build.
