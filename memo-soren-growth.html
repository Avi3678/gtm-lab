Soren / Growth

Sell the first workflow before the whole transformation.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: Soren's readiness assessment gives growth a more concrete entry point than a broad AI transformation promise.

What I noticed

Soren's introduction says most engagements begin with a one-week assessment of automation opportunities, data and security posture, producing a ranked roadmap. It also describes deployment inside infrastructure a client controls. Those details give a prospective customer two useful questions: which workflow is worth changing, and what constraints must the change respect?

I'd test a discovery brief around one workflow, with a named user, current handoffs, data boundaries and a decision the assessment should answer. It would not estimate savings before understanding the baseline. My hypothesis is that a bounded starting point could help the right buyer evaluate an engagement. I cannot infer Soren's sales objections or win rates from its public positioning.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Learn how the team qualifies assessment opportunities. Review approved examples and ask which buyer roles own the workflow, the budget and the security review.

Days 8-20. Draft one industry-specific discovery brief with the team's input. Keep observed facts separate from assumptions, and include reasons an opportunity might not fit. Use public or synthetic examples only.

Days 21-30. Support a small, approved prospect-research test. Track qualified discovery conversations, unanswered questions and agreed next steps. Use feedback to revise the brief rather than promise a full transformation.

At Flywheelr, my AI-SDR work helped gain three clients in two months. I'd bring disciplined research and follow-through, then learn the operational context from Soren's engineers.
