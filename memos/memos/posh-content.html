POSH / Content & growth

Make the next conversation site-specific.

Avi Bedi / November 16, 2026 / Outside-in analysis

My read: POSH's power-first positioning can work hardest when the content helps a buyer describe a real project, rather than simply agree that AI needs more power.

What I noticed

The POSH site starts with existing electrical infrastructure, then describes site selection, the power mix, project control, permits and commissioning. Its invitation to prospective customers asks for location, target IT load and the required online date. That is a much more specific commercial entry point than a general energy-transition narrative.

I'd build the content around the decisions that make those three inputs useful. A site-readiness explainer could distinguish installed electrical infrastructure from power that is actually available to a proposed load. A second piece could explain what still has to be established before an online date is credible. The hypothesis is that useful qualification content can improve the quality of inbound conversations. The public site alone does not establish whether unqualified demand is a problem today.

What I'd do in my first 30 days

Days 1-7. Ask the project team which questions repeatedly need explanation, and which details cannot be discussed publicly. Learn the difference between a candidate site, a secured project and commissioned capacity.

Days 8-20. Write one technical-reviewable site-readiness brief and adapt it into a short LinkedIn series. Connect every version to the same project inquiry; avoid invented speed, cost or carbon claims.

Days 21-30. Test a narrow distribution plan with the team. Review inquiry completeness and the share of inquiries that meet its qualification criteria, alongside the objections raised. Revise before increasing volume.

At Flywheelr I worked on content workflows behind 100K+ LinkedIn impressions. Here I'd treat reach as the entry point, not the outcome: the useful result is a better-informed project conversation.
